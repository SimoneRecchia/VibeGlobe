# Atmosphere and lighting

Survey for milestone M11 (F390), written by the atmosphere track (`docs/internal/parallel-tracks.md`). Decision: [ADR 0040](../adr/0040-atmosphere.md).

## 1. Scope and requirements

The sky, the atmosphere glow around the globe, haze and fog over terrain and imagery, and sun lighting by date and time, with their quality settings. Anti-aliasing, anisotropic filtering, quality presets and adaptive quality (F397–F403) are surveyed by the main session.

Requirements served:

- `PLAN.md`, Horizon and distance: "atmospheric haze or fog hides distant low detail and reduces loads near the horizon; sky and atmosphere when the globe is visible; optional sun lighting by date and time".
- `feature_list.json` F391–F396: sky instead of a flat clear color; a limb glow that changes smoothly with altitude without banding; no jump through the morph and the tilt; haze that hides low-detail tiles near the horizon; fewer tile loads where fully fogged; a terminator at the astronomically correct position (0.1°).
- Engineering standards: the same image on WebGPU and on forced WebGL2 (ADR 0010); no garbage in the frame loop (ADR 0018); the tablet budget (`docs/internal/plan/quality.md`); no black or empty pixels in recorded frames.

## 3. Approaches found

**Sky model.**

- _Analytic sky for a ground observer_ (three.js `Sky`/`SkyMesh`, Babylon's `SkyMaterial`: Preetham). One evaluation per pixel and no planet: no view from space, the horizon color is repeated below the horizon. Unfit for a globe.
- _O'Neil single scattering per vertex_ (osgEarth medium quality, 2 samples; Cesium's ancestor). A polynomial `scale()` replaces the light-path integral, valid only for one scale depth (0.25 of the shell) and a 1.025 radius ratio, with one scale height for Rayleigh and Mie. Per-vertex results show interpolation artifacts on coarse meshes.
- _Ray-marched single scattering_ (CesiumJS): 16 primary × 4 light steps, dropping to 4 × 2 inside the atmosphere through a tanh blend, per vertex of a 256² ellipsoid by default (per fragment on request), float32 meters, phase functions applied afterwards.
- _Precomputed multiple scattering_ (Bruneton and Neyret; osgEarth best quality). 4D-in-3D tables (256×128×32) precomputed with 4 scattering orders; costly to rebuild, so parameters are static; needs compute shaders in osgEarth (GL 4.3); known horizon precision problems in its ray-ground test and in the S(cam) − T·S(point) subtraction used for aerial perspective.
- _Small per-frame LUTs_ (Hillaire 2020; Babylon's addon and OpenGlobus follow it): transmittance 256×64, multiple scattering 32×32, sky view 192×108 (Babylon 128×128), aerial perspective froxels 32×32×32 at 4 km per slice. Babylon falls back from half float to 8-bit LUTs where half-float rendering is missing, a banding risk; OpenGlobus builds 1024² RGBA16F LUTs once and marches 16 samples per sky pixel.

**Haze, fog and aerial perspective.**

- _Distance fog toward a constant color_ (three.js `Fog`, Filament): exponential or exponential-height fog on view depth or radial distance. The fog color is matched to the sky by hand (three.js examples) or sampled from the sky cubemap (Filament's `fogColorFromIbl`), which is the only way the horizon matches.
- _Fog from the scattering model_ (Cesium's ground atmosphere, OpenGlobus' and Babylon's aerial perspective): the same model drives sky and fog, so the horizon matches by construction. Cesium uses two different light intensities for sky (50) and ground (10), and its issue tracker shows the cost of that divergence (#13897, PR #11744).

**Fog and tile loading.** Only CesiumJS ties fog to level of detail: `fog(d, ρ) = 1 − exp(−(dρ)²)`, a tile is culled when the factor reaches 1, and its screen-space error is reduced by `fog × 2` (`QuadtreePrimitive.js`); 3D Tiles use the same scheme with a factor of 24. The density is a function of camera height, multiplied by `1 − |view·up|` so fog only appears when tilting toward the horizon, and switched off above 800 km and outside the 3D view. OpenGlobus and osgEarth leave level of detail untouched.

**Sun position.** Cesium uses Simon et al. (1994) planetary positions through the ICRF→fixed transform (tested against STK to 1e-3); OpenGlobus and osgEarth use Schlyter's simplified elements (about an arcminute). For a terminator good to 0.1°, the low-precision solar coordinates of Meeus (ch. 25, about 0.01°) and Greenwich mean sidereal time (ch. 12) suffice; nutation in right ascension, ΔT and polar motion each move the result by less than 0.01°.

**Lighting and the terminator.** Cesium shades `clamp(N·L × 5 + 0.3)`, a soft terminator, and fades lighting out as the camera nears the ground (`lightingFadeOutDistance`); osgEarth gates direct sun with a ±0.6° smoothstep; OpenGlobus replaces the Sun by a camera-following light below 12,079 km and slerps back by a fixed amount per frame, which ties the transition speed to the frame rate (#584).

**Banding.** Cesium, osgEarth and Babylon's atmosphere add no dithering. OpenGlobus adds interleaved gradient noise of ±0.5/255 after tone mapping; Filament adds triangle noise of ±1/255 after tone mapping (highp, for an Adreno bug) and turns it off in its golden-image tests.

**Globe-to-flat continuity.** Cesium draws the sky only in 3D and during the morph, fading it with `smoothstep(morphTime)`, and switches fog off outside 3D: a hard switch for fog and for its tile culling. osgEarth's sky does not support projected maps. OpenGlobus has no flat mode.

## 5. How they test it

- CesiumJS: numeric specs for fog density (`FogSpec.js`), tile culling in full fog (`GlobeSurfaceTileProviderSpec.js`), the ephemeris against STK; the sky's draw command is checked but not its pixels ("not reliable enough across browsers", `SkyAtmosphereSpec.js`).
- Babylon.js: eight reference-image tests of the atmosphere (day, sunset, night, space view, ray-march vs LUT) on WebGL2 and WebGPU in its Playwright visualization suite.
- three.js: Puppeteer screenshots of every example, including both sky examples, with a 0.1% pixel tolerance.
- Filament: golden images headless on Mesa and SwiftShader in Chromium, with dithering off; no test covers fog.
- OpenGlobus: a unit test that the opacity parameters interpolate linearly with altitude; osgEarth: a GL-error smoke test.

No project measures banding or frame-to-frame continuity.

## 6. Approach chosen

[ADR 0040](../adr/0040-atmosphere.md), in short:

- **One single-scattering model for sky and haze**, ray-marched per pixel in the shader (sky) and per surface fragment (haze, aerial perspective), Rayleigh and Mie in exponential layers with Bruneton's and Hillaire's constants (scale heights 8 and 1.2 km, Mie g = 0.8). The light path's optical depth is an analytic Chapman function (erfc form with the Abramowitz-Stegun bound, through the tangent point below the horizontal), so there is no inner loop and no LUT to build, store, filter or restore after a device loss. Sky and haze share every constant and the display mapping, so the horizon matches by construction.
- **Precision by construction**: altitudes along a ray are computed as h0 + (r_d² − r0²)/(r_d + r0) and ray-sphere crossings in the stable quadratic form, so nothing subtracts the planet radius from a distance to the center. The eye's altitude comes from the CPU in float64. The WGS84 ellipsoid becomes a sphere by scaling the polar axis by a/b, which also maps the ellipsoid's normals, so the terminator falls exactly where N·L = 0 on the ellipsoid.
- **Continuity**: the morph to flat grows the sphere's radius as R / (1 − t)², so curvature fades to nothing with no switch; the same formulas hold for any radius. Inside and outside the atmosphere use one code path (the ray starts where it enters). Without sun lighting the light stands behind the eye, so the visible side is always lit and nothing switches with altitude.
- **Banding**: ±1 step of triangular 8-bit noise in a gamma-2 space after the display mapping, in the sky and haze shaders.
- **Fog-aware detail**: `surfaceVisibility` gives the luminance-weighted transmittance from the eye to a point; the engine multiplies a tile's screen-space error by it, so a tile seen through haze refines less and a fully hazed one stays at its coarse level (Cesium's idea, with the physical transmittance instead of a separate density).
- **Sun position**: Meeus' low-precision solar coordinates and sidereal time, tested against Astronomy Engine to 0.02°.
- **Quality**: sample counts per preset (sky 8–24, haze 4–12) are uniforms, so a preset switch rebuilds no pipeline.

Rejected for now: precomputed multiple scattering (Bruneton) for its precompute cost and static parameters; Hillaire's per-frame LUTs, which would cut the sky's per-pixel cost on tablets but add render targets and half-float requirements on WebGL2 — the fallback if the tablet budget is not met (`docs/internal/follow-ups.md`).

**How it is tested** (`packages/renderer/src/atmosphere/*.test.ts`, `e2e/atmosphere.spec.ts` on the harness page `e2e/support/atmosphere.html`, both backends): the Chapman approximation against brute-force integration (within 6%); altitude and crossing formulas at the globe's radius and at a million times it; the ephemeris against reference positions; screenshots of the canonical views against baselines and across backends; no dark pixels inside the atmosphere; temporal glitch detection (no frame step larger than its neighbors by more than 3 levels) through a flight from orbit to the ground, the tilt and the morph; a banding measure (longest flat run per column) with and without dithering; the brightness step across a tile boundary near the camera and near the horizon at the maximum tilt; the terminator's screen position against the computed sub-solar point.
